Hello Spaceship abuse team;

I’m reporting a domain you sponsor, ao3.life, for large-scale copyright infringement, impersonation of the Archive of Our Own (AO3) / the Organization for Transformative Works (OTW), and a deceptive, bad-faith content-removal process. I am an author and a member of the writing community being harmed. I’m not affiliated with the site.

*Timing/Registration (aka opportunistic abuse)*
- Domain: ao3.life - archiveorourown.org has been offline since 2026-10-29. ao3.life was registered through Spaceship three days later on 2026-11-01, to capitalize on that outage while authors can’t access or manage their own works. The registrant has privacy enabled.
- The site markets itself as a “rescue mirror” headlined “AO3, saved before it slips away” and invites users to “Browse, read and download, no account needed.” It is exploiting a real service’s outage to harvest and republish that service’s content.

*Mass copyright infringement for commercial gain*
- ao3.life republishes fanfiction works scraped from Archive of Our Own without the authors’ permission. It displays AO3-style titles, authors, word/chapter counts, kudos, and tags. It uses “ao3:” work IDs, offering read/epub/audio of each work.
- It is _monetizing_ those works. The page at /revenue-share states, verbatim: "After the cost of making the audiobook (narration and processing), all profits are shared 91% to the author, 9% to ao3.life.” The site sells audiobooks derived from third parties’ copyrighted writing and keeps a cut. Authors did not consent to the hosting, the derivative audiobooks, or the sale.
- Multiple affected authors have publicly confirmed their complete works are present without permission (see evidence links below).

*Impersonation of AO3/OTW*
- The site trades on the “AO3” name and the Archive of Our Own identity throughout its branding and headline to appear to be an official or sanctioned continuation of the Archive. A footer line does not cure the consumer confusion it deliberately creates. AO3 is operated by the OTW, a registered nonprofit - this is unauthorized use of their name and goodwill.

*Deceptive, bad-faith takedown process that collects victims’ AO3 identities*
- The site tells authors their work will be removed if they email legal@ao3.life. In practice, both removal and any payout are gated on the author contacting the operator _from the email address on their AO3 account_ and posting a verification note on their public AO3 profile (per the /revenue-share page).
- This does three things:
  1. Conditions the removal of infringing content on victims performing identity actions
  2. Harvests and correlates authors’ real AO3 account emails with their pen names
  3. By multiple accounts may not be resulting in actual removals.
- It also asserts the operator keeps any “earnings” even after a work is removed. This is a deceptive practice and a data-collection vector targeting the very people it is infringing.
